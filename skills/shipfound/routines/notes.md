# Routine: notes

Build in public notes: the founder's own posts on X and Reddit about their product. A note starts from something real that happened: a milestone in their analytics, a launch, a feature they shipped, a free tool that went live, a week of work on the product. You write it in their voice, learned from their own posts, with an image of the real thing, and fill it in for them. They post it.

A feature is told from the problem it solves: what was hard for the people using the product before, what shipped, how it works now. That is a better post than "here is my product", and it is the founder's own story.

The long form is a flagship X Article: the story of one source told in full, with a designed cover and images of the real product, at most one every 14 days ("The X Article", below). Articles need X Premium; on an account without it the same story goes out as an X thread.

Input: a source key from grow (`milestone:...`, `launch:...`, `feature:...`, `tool:...`, `skill:...`, `repo:...`), `article` with or without a source key for the long form, `voice` to learn their voice again, or nothing (then the first source, or what the founder tells you happened).

## Hard lines here

- **Their product, never Shipfound's work.** A note is about what the founder built, shipped, launched or learned. Never a site fix, a content page, an index request, a visibility run or a listing, and never "my agent wrote 2 pages" or "named in 0 of 4 ChatGPT runs". A status report about Shipfound's work tells their followers nothing they can use. Do not name Shipfound unless the founder asks.
- **Never the people who asked, in the note.** A feature built from a feature idea came from people's public posts. Never name, quote, link or tag them in a post, and never write "a Redditor asked". "People kept asking for X" only when the source's facts say people asked. Telling them it shipped is a reply in their own thread (step 3), never a post about them.
- **Real numbers only.** A number comes from the source's `facts` or from the founder, and the founder decides whether it is shared. Ask before a number goes in a public post ("104 signups", or "passed 100", or none).
- **They post it.** On X you fill the composer in their Chrome and stop. On Reddit, which Claude cannot open, the draft waits on Assisted posts with Reddit's submit page filled in. Never press post.
- **A real image, never generated.** A screenshot of the real product, a still from the product video, or a card made in code with their own numbers. Never AI images, stock photos, or a customer's real data in a screenshot.
- **No engagement bait.** No "like if you agree", no "comment for the link", no tagging big accounts, no more hashtags than their own posts use.

## Procedure

1. Call `workspace` with `repo`. Count this week's work on the product, if you are in its repo: read the commit subjects on the default branch (`git log --since="7 days ago" --no-merges --format=%s`) and count the ones that change the product. Leave out Shipfound's own work: commits from its PRs (branches named `shipfound/...`, subjects like `feat(seo)`, `feat(content)`, `chore(tracking)`; see references/pr-conventions.md). Then the features: the founder's own merged PRs and `feat:` commits of the last 14 days (`git log --since="14 days ago" --no-merges --format=%s`, and `gh pr list --state merged --limit 20` when gh works) that change what the person using the product can do. Up to 5, each as `{ title, problem?, url? }`: the title says what it does for them, the problem only when the code or the PR says what was hard before, the url the PR. Never a refactor, a fix nobody would notice, or Shipfound's own work.
2. Call `notes_context` (free) with `repoCommits` and `features`. It returns the `sources` not written yet, each with its open `platforms`, the founder's `voice` per platform, and the `subreddits` that take these posts. If no platform is open, say that in one line and stop. If there is no source on record (the founder picked a note from the full list), ask them in one question what happened with their product that they would post about; their answer is the source (`founder:<YYYY-MM-DD>-<a few words>`, its facts only what they said). Nothing to share: say so and stop. Never make up a note to fill the gap.
3. **Pick the source:** the one in the input, else the first. Say in one line what the note is about and where it goes ("Your first payment, on X and Reddit").
   - **A feature with `askers`** (built from a feature idea; the people who asked can still be told): ask one question first, header "Tell people": "Post and replies (Recommended)", the note and a reply to each of the N people who asked; "Only the post"; "Only the replies"; "Not now". Replies: routines/ideas.md step 10, with the source's `idea` key. The post: carry on below.
   - **A free tool now live:** ask the same way, the replies being X conversations where people ask for what the tool does: search X in the founder's Chrome for the tool's words (the last two weeks, "Latest"), keep at most three posts where someone asks for it outright, and draft a reply to each as routines/reach.md step 4 says, with the tool's link. Never Reddit here: Claude cannot open it.
4. **Voice**, for each platform the note goes to that has no stored voice, or for every platform when the input is `voice`. Learn it once; after that `notes_context` returns it.
   - **X:** open their own profile in their Chrome (`x.com/<handle>`: `access` has it in the x area's facts, or ask). Read their last 30 to 50 posts of their own, not replies or reposts, scrolling as needed. Note how long they are, casing, punctuation, emoji, hashtags, line breaks, how they open, whether they write threads, whether they link, the words and phrases they come back to, and what they talk about.
   - **Reddit:** Claude cannot open Reddit. Ask the founder to paste two to five of their own Reddit posts or comments. If they have none or would rather not, write Reddit in their X voice with Reddit's habits (full sentences, no hashtags, longer), and say so.
   - Fewer than 10 posts of their own on X: say so, and use the Voice section of SKILL.md until they have more.
   - Save it with `notes_voice`: `sample` the number of posts read, a `summary` in a few lines, `rules` one line each ("all lowercase", "no emoji", "short lines with a blank line between"), and up to five `examples`, verbatim.
5. **Gather the material.**
   - Milestone: the number from `facts`, and what led to it if the founder says.
   - A feature: the problem from `facts` (or ask the founder what was hard before), then read the code it changed and say what someone using the product can do now that they could not, in their terms. The image is the feature in use.
   - Launch, free tool or skill: the post or page in `facts`. Open it and read what it says.
   - A week's work: the commits you counted, and the code they change. Say what changed for the person using the product, not the files.
   - Ask the founder one question for the part only they know: what was hard, what surprised them, what is next. One line from them makes the note theirs. Use their words.
6. **Write the drafts**, one per open platform for this source:
   - **In their voice.** Their stored rules beat the Voice section of SKILL.md on casing, emoji, length and tone. These house rules stay: no made-up numbers, none of the banned words, no em dashes.
   - **X:** one post, at most 280 characters as X counts them (each link is 23). Lead with what happened. A feature leads with the problem, then what shipped ("clients ignore invoice emails. so now [product] sends the reminder on WhatsApp"), in their voice. A link only when the source has one worth opening (the launch, the tool, the product), to their own site or the launch post.
   - **Reddit:** pick the subreddit from `subreddits` that fits: r/SideProject for a launch or a tool, r/indiehackers or r/EntrepreneurRideAlong for a milestone with what they learned, r/buildinpublic for progress. Read its `rules` and `gate` and keep every one. A plain title (what happened, with the number if they share it), never clickbait. The body: what happened, how, what they learned, what is next, and one honest question for the sub. A link only where the rules allow it; when unsure, none. Before their first post in a sub, ask them to check its account age and karma minimums (SKILL.md, "Reddit: you post it").
7. **The image**, one per note, saved under `.shipfound/notes/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<slug>/` in the repo root (add `.shipfound/` to `.git/info/exclude` if it is not there):
   - A launch, a tool or a week's work: a screenshot of the real screen it is about. Use the video kit's capture (routines/video.md, step 4: `shots.json` with that page, then `node capture.mjs`), or a frame of the product video if there is one (`npx remotion still Square <file> --frame=<n>` in `.shipfound/video/`). Use a demo account, never a customer's data.
   - A milestone: a card made in code. A small HTML page in their brand (the logo, fonts and colors the capture reads) with the number and what it counts, nothing else, then `npx playwright screenshot --viewport-size=1200,675 file://<card.html> x.png`.
   - Look at it before you use it: nothing cut off, nothing private, readable on a phone.
8. **Show the founder** each draft and the image. In Claude Code, one AskUserQuestion per platform: "Use it (Recommended)", "Edit it", "Leave <platform> out". Their edits go in as they wrote them.
9. **Save** with `notes_save` (free): `source` its key and title, `x` and or `reddit` with the text and the image path. The source is written now and is never offered again. Saving again for the same source revises a draft they have not posted.
10. **Fill it in.**
    - **X:** open the `openUrl` it returns in their Chrome (X's composer with the post in it), attach the image, and stop. They read it and post it.
    - **Reddit:** give them the Assisted posts link and the image path. The draft there opens Reddit's submit page with the title and text filled in; they attach the image if the sub takes one, and post.
11. End with "Tell me when you have posted it" and stop. When they give the links, call `reach_mark` with `status` posted and the `url` for each (ids from `notes_save`), so each is recorded. A note they leave out: `reach_mark` with `status` skipped. Then the hand-over (SKILL.md), once they say they have posted.

A note is a few minutes' work and grow offers one at most every 3 days on X and every 14 on Reddit. Never offer a second note in the same sitting.

## The X Article

The input is `article` (from grow, `article <source key>`; or the founder asks for an Article or a long post). One every 14 days, on real news only: a feature, a launch, a milestone, a free tool or a skill, never a week of commits.

1. Steps 1 and 2 above, then read `article` from `notes_context`: `open`, `next`, `premium`, `format` and its `sources`. Not open: say when it is (`next`) and stop. No source: ask the founder in one question what they would write the story of (step 2's founder source). Take the source in the input, else the first of `article.sources`.
2. **Premium.** `premium` false: it is a thread (step 6). Null: open https://x.com/compose/articles in their Chrome. The Articles editor (a Write button, or drafts) means Premium; an offer to subscribe means none, so it is a thread. Pass what you saw as `premium` to `notes_save`. True: an Article.
3. **The voice** is their X voice (step 4 above). An Article is longer than their posts, never stiffer: keep their casing, their words, their length of sentence.
4. **The story**, 800 to 1,800 words, from what is true: the source's facts, the code, the founder's answers. Ask them two questions first: what was hard about it, and what they would tell someone building the same thing. Shape: a title that says what happened (under 100 characters, never a question or a tease), a first paragraph that says the whole thing, then the problem as people had it, what was built and why that way, how it works (with the images), what they learned, what is next, and one link to the product or the feature. Headings every few paragraphs; short paragraphs; no list longer than five. The house rules hold: no made-up numbers, no banned words, no em dashes.
5. **The design**, made in code from the real product, never AI images or stock. Read the brand the video kit captures (routines/video.md, step 4: `brand.json` with colors and fonts, and the logo) if `.shipfound/video/public/brand/` has none yet.
   - **The cover**, 1500 by 600 (5:2): a small HTML page in their brand with the title in their heading font and a real screen of the product (a capture of the page the story is about), then `npx playwright screenshot --viewport-size=1500,600 file://<cover.html> cover.png`.
   - **Two to five images**, 1200 by 675: the real screens the story walks through (captured with the video kit's `shots.json` and `node capture.mjs`, in a demo account, never a customer's data), each framed on a plain background in their brand with a one-line caption; and when the story has a real number the founder agreed to share, a card with it.
   - Look at every image before you use it: nothing cut off, nothing private, readable on a phone. Save them under `.shipfound/notes/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<slug>/` and copy them to `~/Desktop/<product>-article-<YYYY-MM-DD>/`, so the founder has them too.
   - In the body, put each image on its own line as `![its caption](<its path>)` where it goes.
6. **Without Premium, the thread**: the same story in 5 to 8 posts of at most 280 characters as X counts them: the first says the whole thing and makes a stranger want the rest, the last has the one link. The cover and the images go on the posts they belong to (at most one each).
7. **Show the founder** the title, the first paragraph, the outline and the images (or every post of the thread). In Claude Code, one AskUserQuestion: "Use it (Recommended)", "Edit it", "Not now". Their edits go in as they wrote them.
8. **Save** with `notes_save` on its own: `source`, then `article` (`title`, `body`, `cover`, `images`) or `thread` (`posts`, each with its `image`), and `premium` when you looked in step 2. It is on its own 14-day clock, apart from the short notes, and the source can still have a short note.
9. **Fill it in on X**, in their Chrome. An Article: open the `openUrl` (X's Articles tab), start a new one, upload the cover, type the title, then the body section by section, with the headings, bold and lists in X's editor and each image uploaded where its line is. A thread: open the composer, type the first post, add each next one with the + button, each with its image. Stop before Publish (or Post all): they read it and post it. Then step 11 above.
