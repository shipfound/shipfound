# Routine: video

A short video of the product for a boost: its screens recreated in HTML and animated, in the founder's own branding, with no sound. One cut per ad, in the three shapes the feeds use, plus an image of each. You build it in code on their machine; nothing is recorded, screenshotted into the video or generated by an AI model. Free: nothing runs on Shipfound's servers.

Input: `redo`, `ads`, or nothing. A boost (routines/boost.md) starts this routine when it has no video, and with `ads` once its ads are written, to make a cut for each.

## What it is

- **Recreated, then animated.** Each scene is one screen of the product rebuilt in HTML and CSS from its own source: the same layout, labels, copy and states. Then it moves the way the product does: what the user does (typing, a click) and what the product does in answer (results coming in, a number counting up), with the camera moving in on the part that matters. Built with Remotion from the kit in the plugin's [video/](../../../video/) folder.
- **Built for a stranger in a feed.** The viewer knows nothing about the product and does not care yet. The order is **pain or outcome, then proof, then how**: the hook (2 seconds), 1 or 2 scenes of what the buyer gets (the outcome on screen), one scene of how (the single action the user takes), the end card (2.5 seconds). 15 to 20 seconds is right; 30 is the most. Setup screens (settings, onboarding, an audit, a pull request opening) do not go in: they mean nothing to someone who has not bought it.
- **Frame 0 is the ad.** The first frame is the thumbnail and all a scroller sees before the video plays, so the hook is whole on frame 0: big, no fade-in, no word-by-word entrance, the logo under it. A slow push-in is its only motion.
- **One cut per ad, three shapes each.** Each ad opens on its own hook, so the same video never runs twice. Each cut renders as Square 1:1 (1200x1200: X, Reddit), Portrait 4:5 (1080x1350: Meta feeds) and Vertical 9:16 (1080x1920: Reels and Stories; the screen and caption sit in the middle, clear of the app's own overlays). Plus an image of each ad, 4:5 and 1:1: its hook as the headline, one screen at its finished frame, the payoff line, the logo, the small print and the domain. The image runs beside the video as a second creative, and in its place where a platform rejects the video upload.
- **Their brand.** The capture reads the landing page: the logo as its header shows it, the font files its headings and body text use, and its background, text, muted and accent colors. The hook and end card carry the logo; captions use their heading font.
- **Only the product, no sound.** No music, voiceover or sound effects: each file carries a silent audio track only (an upload that expects audio gets one). Never a screen recording, never the founder's own clip, never AI video or images, no stock footage, no AI people.
- **On the Desktop.** The finished files go in a folder on the founder's Desktop, `<product>-ads-<date>`, named by ad (`ad1-video-portrait.mp4`, `ad1-image-square.png`), never only in the hidden `.shipfound/` folder: the founder drags them into upload boxes themselves, and on Reddit every step is theirs.

## The words: the stranger test

Every word on screen passes this before the founder sees a cut. Look from the customer's side: they know nothing about the product, and they do not care. Take the product's line and ask "so what?" three times, out loud, until you reach what they feel: "We do growth for you" → so what? → "people find your product" → so what? → "they sign up" → so what? → "the thing you built finally has users, and you did not have to become a marketer." The video opens on that last rung: the pain or the outcome, in the buyer's words. The product's name and how it works come after.

- **The hook:** 8 words or fewer, the ad's own first line or the bottom of its ladder ("You built it. Nobody came."). Never how the product works, and never a line that needs the reader to know the product already.
- **Captions** say what the viewer gets, 7 words or fewer: "Now people find your product.", not "First, it checks your access." The test: each caption makes sense with the sound off and the screen ignored. The kit refuses a longer hook or caption.
- **The end card** is a promise plus a free next step from their landing page ("Free to start. No card.") and the bare domain. Never a task ("Install it. Run the audit.").
- **Never in a hook, caption or headline:** the product's own vocabulary and internal mechanics (its feature names, settings, statuses, credits, pull requests, any term a buyer would have to look up).

## Hard lines here

- **True to the product.** Recreate only screens and steps the product really has, with its own labels and wording. Simplify (drop a sidebar, shorten a list) but never add a feature, a button or a result it does not have. Every number on screen is demo data, plausible for the product, never presented as a customer's result.
- **No one's real data.** Demo names, emails and sites only (`demo-shop.example`, "Acme"), never a customer, a real person, a key or a token, even if the reference shot shows one.
- Never type the founder's password. If the reference shots need the live app, they sign in by hand in the window the capture opens.
- Every word in a caption, the hook, the image and the end card comes from the ad being made, the post being boosted or the landing page, rewritten only to pass the stranger test, and the founder approves it. A caption names what the scene shows.
- The files stay on their machine (the kit folder and the Desktop folder) until they attach them to a post or an ad themselves.

## Procedure

1. **Is there one already?** With input `ads`, go to "A cut for each ad" below. In the root of their repo, look for `.shipfound/video/out/video-1x1.mp4`. If it is there and under 30 days old, and the input is not `redo`, show them `out/sheet.png` and ask whether to use it or make a new one. Using it: done, go back to the caller. A video made before this routine had cuts (its hook fades in, or it opens on a setup screen) is remade: say why in one line.
2. **Set up the kit**, in `.shipfound/video/` at the root of their repo: copy the plugin's [video/](../../../video/) folder there (if the skill was copied on its own, fetch it from https://github.com/shipfound/shipfound/tree/main/video). Add `.shipfound/` to `.git/info/exclude`, so it is never committed and their `.gitignore` is not touched. Then `npm install` and `npx playwright install chromium` in that folder (a few hundred MB, once). If installing is blocked (no network, a sandbox), say what to run and stop. A kit copied before this version is updated: copy `src/Clip.tsx`, `src/Root.tsx`, `src/Static.tsx`, `ads.mjs` and `package.json` over it, keeping their `src/scenes/` and `public/`.
3. **Pick the story.** Write the so-what ladder for the product (above), then the scenes in the order pain or outcome → how: 1 or 2 outcome scenes (what the buyer's world looks like once it works: traffic rising, a list filling, a result appearing) and one "how" scene (the single action the user takes). Take them from the landing page and the ad. Say it to the founder in one line per scene, with the hook, and let them change it.
4. **Read the real thing** for each scene, as many of these as you have:
   - **The source in their repo:** the components, pages and styles that render the screen (its markup, class names, colors, spacing) and the exact copy. For a CLI or an agent, the code that prints its output, so the text on screen is the text it prints.
   - **Reference shots:** write `shots.json` (see `shots.example.json`) with a shot per screen and run `node capture.mjs`. It also reads the brand. It saves `ref/<name>.png` and the screen's text in `ref/<name>.txt`; look at each. For the live app, add `login`: the founder signs in by hand in the window that opens; ask them to use a demo account if they have one. With no screens to shoot (a CLI), keep `shots` empty: it reads the brand only.
   - Their docs or README, for the real commands and flows.
5. **Check the brand** before building on it. `public/brand/logo.png` must be their whole logo and nothing else (not a menu icon, not cut off); if not, set `brand.logo` in shots.json to the right selector and run it again. `capture.mjs` prints the fonts and colors it found; a font "not found" falls back to Inter, so say so.
6. **Build each scene** in `src/scenes/<id>.tsx` and register it in `src/scenes/index.ts` (start from `browser-example.tsx` or `terminal-example.tsx`, then delete the examples). The stage is 1160x900; `BrowserWindow` or `TerminalWindow` frames it. Recreate the screen to match the reference: layout, labels, colors (the app's own, from its source), type. Animate it with the kit (`src/kit.tsx`): `Typing` for what the user enters, `Cursor` with `clicks` for what they press, `Appear` for what the product returns, `CountUp` for a number, `Highlight` and `Camera` for the part that matters. Phone-sized rules: text at least 30px on the stage (34px is the terminal default), at most 6 lines or rows in view, one idea per scene, everything moving or done by half the scene. The stage is shown smaller on the tall shapes, so leave the smallest text bigger than you would for a square.

   **No flicker.** Frames are rendered out of order across several browser tabs, so anything not set by the frame number alone flickers. Everything on screen is a function of `useCurrentFrame()`: no CSS animations or transitions, no `Math.random()` or `Date` (fixed demo data instead), no timers, no state that changes on its own, and nothing that blinks on and off (a caret, a dot, a badge): to a viewer, blinking is a glitch. Move things with the kit (`Appear`, `Camera`, `CountUp`) or `interpolate` with clamped ends; for a spring use `settle()` from the kit, never `spring()` directly (a raw spring never quite stops, so text shifts a fraction of a pixel every frame). Put labels and numbers in HTML; avoid SVG `<text>`, which Chrome lays out again at every scale.
7. **Write `public/video.json`** (see `video.example.json`): `hook` (8 words or fewer, the stranger test); `scenes` in order, each with its `id`, `seconds` (3 to 6) and a caption of 7 words or fewer saying what the viewer gets; `image`, the image ad: `hook` with a `\n` where the headline should break, `scene`, `at` (seconds into that scene once it reads whole: no zoom cropping it) and `line`, the payoff; `end`, a promise plus a free next step from the landing page and the bare domain. Leave out `colors`: they come from the brand. This is ad 1's cut; `ads` holds the others ("A cut for each ad").
8. **Check the cut.** Run `npm run thumbs` and look at `out/thumbs.png`: frame 0 of every cut in every shape at 200px wide. If you cannot read a hook there, it fails. Run `npm run sheet` and look at `out/sheet.png`: a frame every half second of the square, with the safe zone drawn in red (`npx remotion still Sheet out/sheet-2.png --props='{"cut":"2"}'` for ad 2). Then render a few frames at full size, `npx remotion still Square out/f.png --frame=<n>` (and `Vertical-1` for the tall layout), one per scene at its busiest moment, and put each next to its reference. Fix and check again if:
   - frame 0 is not the whole hook, readable at 200px wide
   - a hook, caption or end card fails the stranger test, or names how the product works before what the viewer gets
   - a scene does not match the real screen: a wrong label, a missing part, a color or font that is off
   - text is cut off (a zoom that crops a line someone is reading), overlaps, or is too small to read on a phone
   - a caption is outside the red box or covers what matters; on the tall shapes, anything in the top or bottom seventh of the frame, where the app's own buttons sit
   - a frame is blank, or something pops in with no motion, or the cursor clicks on nothing
   - anything shows a real person, customer, email, key or token
   - it runs over 30 seconds
   - anything flickers: see step 9

   At most 2 rounds of fixes; if it is still not right, say exactly what is wrong, show nothing, and stop.
9. **Render, then check for flicker** in the rendered file, where it shows (stills render one frame at a time and hide it). `npm run render`, `cp out/video-1x1.mp4 public/check.mp4`, then for each scene, once its motion has landed: `npx remotion still Frames out/frames.png --props='{"from":<frame>,"x":<x>,"y":<y>,"w":<w>,"h":<h>}'`, 12 consecutive frames zoomed on the scene's smallest text, numbers and labels (a box of the 1200x1200 cut). Everything that is not meant to move must be identical in all 12: a digit that shifts, a label that jumps or fades and returns is a flicker. Fix its cause (the rules in step 6) and render again.
10. **Put it on the Desktop.** `npm run ads`: every cut in every shape and its images, into `~/Desktop/<product>-ads-<date>/` (or `out/ads/` when there is no Desktop folder; it prints where). Look at each image (`ad<n>-image-portrait.png`) against step 8's list. Show them `out/thumbs.png` and the folder's path, and list its files. Ask if they want a scene, a caption or the image changed; a change is steps 6 to 10 again, flicker check included.
11. If a boost started this routine, go back to it. Otherwise close as SKILL.md says, with 0 credits.

## A cut for each ad

Input `ads`, from a boost whose ads are written and approved. The kit, the brand and the scenes are built (steps 2 to 6); each ad after the first gets its own cut of them, so the same video does not run on two ads and the test compares whole ads. If there is no video yet, make it first (steps 1 to 9), with ad 1's message as its hook.

1. **Ad 1** is `video.json`'s own `hook`, `scenes` and `image`: set its hook to ad 1's first line or the bottom rung of its ladder, 8 words or fewer.
2. **Each other ad** is an entry in `ads`, in order (ad 2 first): its own `hook` from its own message; its `scenes` led by the one that shows what that ad's message is about (an ad about the outcome opens on the outcome; one about ease opens on the single action), with captions from that message where they fit; its own `image`. When the message is about a part of the product the scenes do not show, build one or two new scenes for it (steps 3 to 6, the same rules). The product, colors and end card are shared.
3. **Check and render** as steps 8 to 10: every hook on frame 0 in `out/thumbs.png`, each cut's sheet, the flicker check on ad 1's square, then `npm run ads` (`npm run ads -- --ad 2` redoes one ad). Show them the thumbs and the Desktop folder, and go back to the boost with each ad's files: `ad<n>-video-square.mp4`, `ad<n>-video-portrait.mp4`, `ad<n>-video-vertical.mp4`, `ad<n>-image-portrait.png`, `ad<n>-image-square.png`.
